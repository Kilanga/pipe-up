# Feedback from adults who stutter — plan for this week

**Where things stand:** Pipe Up has not yet been tested with people who stutter. The goal this week is modest: 1 to 3 honest reactions from adults who stutter, before the submission closes on November 30, 2026 (2:00 PM Pacific). This is informal product feedback. It is not research, and it must never be presented as research.

## Ground rules

- **Adults only** (18 or over). If someone's age is unclear, ask, and stop if they are not an adult.
- **Ask, don't recruit.** One message, one polite reminder at most. A "no" or a silence is an answer.
- **Consent first.** Say who you are, what the project is, that it is a contest entry, and what you will do with the reply. Say whether you stutter yourself; do not let people assume.
- **No recording without agreement.** Written replies are the default. If someone prefers a call, do not record it; take notes, and read them back at the end.
- **No health data kept.** Do not ask about diagnosis, severity, therapy or history. If someone volunteers it, do not write it down. Keep only what they said about the product.
- **Keep as little as possible.** Store each reaction as: date, "adult who stutters", country if offered, what they said about the product. No name, no email, no handle next to it. That someone stutters is information about their health: do not keep it attached to an identity. Delete the messages and contact details once the notes are taken, and in any case by December 31, 2026.
- **Nothing in the repository.** Notes stay in a private file. Only the wording below goes into public documents.
- **Quotes need a written yes.** Quote only with explicit written permission for that exact sentence, without a name. Otherwise paraphrase.
- **No payment, no promise.** Say so. Offer to send the result of the contest and to keep them posted, if they want.
- **Respect community rules.** Many groups do not allow unsolicited product posts. Write to the moderators or the organisation first and post only if they agree.

## Where to ask

Ask in this order; stop as soon as three people have answered.

1. **People you already know** who stutter, or a friend of a friend. A personal introduction is the most respectful route and the fastest.
2. **AImpower.org** (https://aimpower.org/) — a nonprofit that builds communication technology "for, with, and by people who stutter", and whose researchers led the co-design study Pipe Up starts from. Write to them as a peer asking for criticism, and cite their work.
3. **National associations**, through their contact form, asking whether a short request may be shared with adult members: STAMMA in the UK (co-author of the survey cited in the README), and the equivalent association in your own country. Associations often answer slowly: send this on day one.
4. **Online communities of adults who stutter**, only with the moderators' agreement.

Sources checked for this list: https://aimpower.org/ and https://www.nbt.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/document/Stammer_Executive_Summary.pdf. Check every other organisation's own site and rules before writing.

## Message, ready to send

Choose one of the two bracketed options in the second sentence; it must be true.

> **Subject: A video-call tool for people who stutter — would you tell me what is wrong with it?**
>
> Hello,
>
> My name is Arnaud. I am a solo developer in France, and [I do not stutter myself / I stutter myself].
>
> For a hackathon, I built Pipe Up: a free, open-source Chrome extension for Google Meet. It is meant as a backup for people who stutter. One key posts "I'm still speaking, one moment." in the chat. It can also suggest short replies taken from notes you prepared, which you send only if you press a key. It does not change or hide anyone's speech.
>
> No one who stutters has tried it yet. I would rather hear now that something is wrong than keep guessing.
>
> Would you spend ten minutes on the demo and tell me what you think? There is nothing to install and no account: https://pipe-up-demo.web.app (click "Load Sam's sample briefing", then "Start the call").
>
> Three questions. Answer any of them, in a few lines:
> 1. Would the "I'm still speaking" key be of use to you in a real meeting?
> 2. The suggested replies: useful, or in the way?
> 3. Is anything in the idea or in the wording off-putting?
>
> How I will use your reply: I will note what you say about the product, without your name. I will not quote you unless you tell me in writing that I may. I am not asking about your health or your speech. Nothing is recorded. I cannot offer payment. If you change your mind, tell me and I will delete your reply.
>
> If the answer is no, thank you for reading this far.
>
> Arnaud

### Short version, to ask a moderator or an association first

> Hello. I am a solo developer and I built a free, open-source Chrome extension for Google Meet, meant as a backup for people who stutter (demo: https://pipe-up-demo.web.app). No one who stutters has tried it yet. May I ask adult members for ten minutes of honest feedback? I am not selling anything, I record nothing and I keep no names. If this kind of request is not welcome here, I will not post. Thank you.

### If someone answers

- Thank them the same day. Do not argue with criticism; ask one clarifying question at most.
- If they offer a call: no recording, 15 minutes, their pace. Do not finish their sentences. Do not ask them to demonstrate anything.
- Ask before quoting: "May I quote this sentence, without your name: '…'?"

## Exact wording for the submission

**If no feedback arrives in time** — use this sentence, unchanged, in the README, the Devpost text and the video:

> Pipe Up has not yet been tested with people who stutter. Its design follows published research, not user testing. Getting that feedback is the first next step.

(The video uses the short form already in the script: "It has not yet been tested with people who stutter. That comes next.")

**If 1 to 3 people answer** — replace it with this, filling in only what is true:

> Between November [D] and [D], 2026, [N] adult[s] who stutter tried the hosted demo and gave informal written feedback. This is not a study. What I heard: [one or two plain sentences, criticism first]. What I changed: [the change, or "nothing yet; it is on the list"].

For the video: "[N] adult[s] who stutter tried the demo this week. This is not a study. Their feedback comes first on my list."

**Never write**, whatever arrives: "validated by", "tested with users" without the number, "people who stutter love", a percentage, a testimonial without written permission, or any claim about speech, fluency, anxiety or health.
